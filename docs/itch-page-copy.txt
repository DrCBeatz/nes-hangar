DESCRIPTION — COPY AND FORMAT IN THE ITCH.IO EDITOR

A small playable experiment: how much of a Doom-like FPS can fit on the NES?

Hangar reimagines a simplified route through Doom's E1M1 using a custom NES
rendering engine. Explore angled walls, pillars, a recessed floor and the
zigzag slime room. Fight three soldiers, manage limited ammunition, collect
supplies and reach the exit.

PLAY IN YOUR BROWSER: https://8bitpocket.app/?game=hangar
Or download the NTSC .nes ROM below and open it in an NES emulator.

This single-level prototype includes a pistol, moving enemies, health and
ammunition pickups, an openable door, pause controls and an end-of-level
results screen. Optional music features Dr C Beatz's NES arrangement of
Bobby Prince's "At Doom's Gate." Select music OFF in the controls guide for
better performance.

For the reference experience, use Mesen in NTSC mode with normal sprite
limits and zero additional scanlines. Complex views render slowly: this is
an experimental NES engine, and feedback on controls, visibility and pacing
is welcome. Browser presentation and speed can differ. The release has been
tested in emulators; physical-console testing is still pending.

NES CONTROLS
Up/Down: walk
Left/Right: turn
A + Left/Right: strafe
B: fire
A: use a nearby door or switch when prompted
Start: advance the title/guide; pause in game; retry after completion/death
Select: credits at the logo; dynamic/coarse wall-boundary comparison in game

The browser displays its keyboard guide before play and provides touch
controls. Default keys: arrows to move/turn, X for A, Z or Y for B, Enter for
Start, Ctrl for Select. Saved custom bindings may differ.

Created by Dr C Beatz with AI-assisted programming, debugging and some visual
asset development, followed by manual editing and repeated playtesting.
The current NES music arrangement was transcribed and authored by Dr C Beatz.

This is an unofficial fan prototype, not a port of the original Doom engine
or the complete original game. Original Doom and E1M1: id Software. Music
composition: Bobby Prince. Music playback: FamiStudio by Mathieu Gauthier,
based on FamiTone2 by Shiru. Content origins and license scope are documented
with the source; the original-code MIT license does not cover all ROM content.
