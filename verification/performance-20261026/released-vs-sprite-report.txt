HANGAR: RELEASED PROTOTYPE VS CURRENT BUILD
Measured 2026-10-26

The latest version is measurably better, with the largest practical change in
initial response when the moving zombie is present at the slime-room entrance.
Fresh rendering is moderately faster; stationary combat benefits much more
from background reuse. Continuous movement still exposes a slow renderer.

Exact versions (no ROM/source changes made for this comparison)

5. Tradeoffs and limits

- 1 scene/music median-interval summary worsens: opening turn OFF
  332.8->349.4ms, even though its whole-window picture rate improves. The
  builds sample different angles at different times during continuous turns.
- 8/26 paired trials spend over 1 ms more CPU on abandoned render attempts,
  up to 246.6 ms extra in a trial. Faster visible response does
  not guarantee less discarded work. The bounded restart policy is retained.
- 4 first-simulation response measurements worsen by approximately 1 ms;
  all tested first-visible and stop responses nevertheless improve.
- Fresh-pose upload byte COUNTS are identical. The latest uploader transfers
  up to 128 pattern bytes in eligible interrupts versus 112 before; sparse
  name transfers remain capped at 96 bytes, with publication/DMA separate.
  Upload and next-frame production overlap; their durations cannot simply
  be added to predict continuous frame rate. Live sampled payload means can
  differ because the versions display different poses along the same route.

Resource                       | Release | Latest
-------------------------------+---------+--------------------------------
Extra background-cache RAM     | 0 bytes | 5 bytes from unused reservation
Free actor-bank bytes          | 127     | 65
Free fixed code/audio bytes    | 20      | 65
Free common-region bytes       | 11      | 2

Some code regions are nearly full. This constrains implementation/layout of

6. Graphics and correctness

190 matched render pairs across 67 legal camera fixtures (172 fresh, 18 warm)
pass. All world pixels/palettes match exactly. 182 pairs also have exact actor
pixels, staging OAM, pattern/name bytes, targeting metadata and hidden-corpse
payloads. 8 differences are due solely to the intentional distant-soldier floor
anchor correction; separate untimed diagnostic substitution restores exact
output. The map's third-encounter activation boundary moved Z128->132 to match
door reach. That behavior fix is documented, not treated as a performance trick.

The 52 primary live trials and 4 reversal trials use 8 isolated Mesen captures.
29,952 recorded frame positions/floor states pass independent map validation.
Both builds have coherent publications, legal upload limits and no observed
PPUDATA writes outside VBlank. The actor-layer CPU oracle is not a hardware
scanout simulation; Mesen supplies the separate display/timing checks. Personal
settings and 164 protected project inputs remain unchanged. Neither version
was tested on a physical console in this comparison.

7. What to investigate next

At the selected latest slime-entry view with a living zombie, geometry setup
and per-column geometry consume 51.9% of producer CPU; wall/floor painting
and tile encoding consume 41.2%. The distant sprite itself is a smaller
component. These are workload shares, not promised optimization percentages.

Keep this build as the measured comparison point. The next focused profile
should split geometry candidate preparation/rejection and per-column work to
find calculations repeated for invisible or already-covered surfaces. After
that, inspect painting/tile encoding if the geometry work proves necessary.
Avoid raising upload limits simply to chase FPS; the existing VBlank budget
needs to remain safe. More sprite work is secondary unless close combat is
the particular feature being targeted. A release-candidate gameplay pass is
reasonable before publishing an update; this benchmark made no deployment.
